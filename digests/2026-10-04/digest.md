## 1. Quantum impurity models: easy at equilibrium, universal in motion

[arXiv:2610.02130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02130) · [SciRate](https://scirate.com/arxiv/2610.02130)

*Srinivasan Arunachalam, Sergey Bravyi, Anirban Chowdhury, Arkopal Dutt, Alexandru Gheorghiu, Zhi Li*

**TL;DR** The authors resolve two open questions about quantum impurity models (a constant-size interacting impurity in a free-fermion bath of n modes): they give a classical algorithm computing the ground energy to additive error ε in poly(n, 1/ε) time (improving on the quasi-polynomial 1/ε dependence of Bravyi–Gosset), extend it to a poly(n, β, 1/ε) algorithm for the free energy and a low-rank Gaussian description of the thermofield double state, and prove that time evolution under a *time-independent* impurity Hamiltonian with only 24 impurity modes is BQP-complete.

**The big picture** Impurity models sit exactly between trivially simulable free fermions and fully interacting systems that are hard even for quantum computers, and they are the workhorse inner loop of dynamical mean-field theory for materials. This work draws a sharp line: everything about equilibrium — ground energy, free energy, thermal expectation values — is classically easy, so there is no quantum advantage to be had there; but real-time dynamics of the very same models is as hard as universal quantum computation. That means proposals to delegate impurity solvers to quantum hardware should target dynamical and spectral quantities, not equilibrium ones, and it also suggests impurity models as a physically natural platform for computation where the program is carried in by incoming particles rather than by external control pulses.

**Key contributions**
- Ground energy in poly(n,1+‖V‖)·exp[O(m log(m/ε))] time, plus a succinct variational state supported on exp[O(m log(m/ε))] Fock configurations.
- A V-independent Gaussian spanning set for the TFD state of size (2+β)^{O(m)}/δ, with no bound on ‖V‖ required.
- A constructive algorithm for the TFD coefficients and free energy in poly(n,β,1/δ) for constant m (assuming ‖V‖≤1).
- BQP-completeness of estimating a single mode occupation after time-independent impurity evolution, with fixed quartic V on 24 modes and n,T = O((k+g)⁴).

**How it works** The bath spectrum is "gapped" artificially by raising all one-particle energies below ω = ε/(4m) to ω, costing only mω in energy because the impurity sees at most m low-energy directions. The spectrum is then binned into O(log(m/ε)) dyadic shells; within each, the m-dimensional impurity-facing boundary A_s and its orthogonal residual bath B_s are split into block-Krylov layers generated by E_s from Im(T_s†). The integrated imaginary-time coupling s(v)=∫‖Te^{-tE}v‖dt decays as ½·3^{-ℓ} at depth ℓ, and a multiparticle pull-through lemma upgrades this to a *product* bound on joint occupations, justifying a weighted Fock truncation with cost ℓ+1 per occupied layer mode. Crucially the truncation projector commutes with V, so the energy error is ‖V‖-independent. The thermal version replaces the vacuum by free-TFD annihilators (thermal Bogoliubov modes) and runs Trotterized imaginary-time evolution; error accumulation is avoided by a global linear least-squares fit over the whole trajectory, with Schatten–Hölder bounds controlling amplification of residuals. The universality construction compiles a circuit into a sequential model where free fermionic wavepackets stream past a fixed constant-size "processor" impurity, combining quantum-walk/scattering computation with a Turing-machine-style autonomous update rule.

**Why it matters** This settles both questions posed by Bravyi–Gosset and gives rigorous backing to the folklore that equilibrium impurity solvers are tractable while long-time dynamics is not. DMFT practitioners and anyone designing hybrid quantum-classical embedding schemes should recalibrate where quantum resources can help.

**Caveats** Runtimes are exponential in the impurity size m (the (m/ε)^{O(m)} factor), so "efficient" means constant m. The constructive TFD result needs ‖V‖≤1, and ground energy needs ‖V‖≤poly(n). The BQP-hardness is for time-independent H but with an n-mode engineered H₀ and a specially prepared Slater determinant; the T=O((k+g)⁴) overhead is substantial. Hardness of the physically central objects — Green's functions and spectral functions at finite temperature — is addressed only in concurrent work, and the gap between easy equilibrium and hard dynamics is not quantified for intermediate observables.

## 2. Local random quantum circuits converge to the Porter-Thomas distribution in polynomial depth

[arXiv:2610.02125](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02125) · [SciRate](https://scirate.com/arxiv/2610.02125)

*Aniruddha Sen, Nicholas Hunter-Jones*

**TL;DR** The authors prove the first rigorous statement that the output probability of a fixed bitstring, as a random variable over brickwork Haar random circuits, converges in total variation distance to the Porter–Thomas (exponential) distribution at polynomial depth: depth $O(n^{2m+1}\log n)$ gives TV distance $O(1/n^m)$. The proof combines $k$-design moment matching, a Hadamard three-circle argument in Fourier space, mollification, and a new inverse-moment/anticoncentration bound on the amplitude mass feeding into a single final-layer gate.

**The big picture** Random circuit sampling experiments and the cross-entropy benchmark used to validate them rest on the assumption that shallow random circuits produce output probabilities obeying the characteristic exponential statistics of fully chaotic dynamics. Until now this was supported only by numerics and by the weaker fact that finitely many moments match — and matching any finite number of moments provably does not pin down a distribution. This work closes the gap for brickwork circuits, showing genuine convergence in a strong distance measure at polynomial depth, by exploiting circuit structure in addition to moment information.

**Key contributions**
- A rigorous convergence theorem: depth-$\ell$ brickwork circuits forming $d^{-k}$-approximate $k$-designs have $d_{\rm TV} \le O(\sqrt{\log k/k})$ from Porter–Thomas; with $k=\Theta(n^{2m}\log n)$ and $\ell=\Theta(n^{2m+1}\log n)$ this is $O(1/n^m)$.
- A technique for upgrading moment control to TV control, circumventing the Hausdorff-moment-problem obstruction, via a three-region Fourier decomposition.
- A smoothness bound $\|f'\|_{L^1}=O(d)$ for the output-probability density, reduced to an inverse-moment bound $\mathbb{E}[R^{-1}]=O(d)$ on the mass $R$ entering the final gate.
- A local anticoncentration statement $\Pr[R\le t/d]\le A t^{12/11}$, proved by iteratively "freeing" qubits through the last two layers and bounding negative moments of $\mathrm{Beta}(2,2)$ ratios ($M_{3/2}=8$ at $q=3/2$).

**How it works** Using a Bobkov-type inequality, TV distance is bounded by the $L^2$ norm of the difference of characteristic functions, with support length $L=2k/d$ from known tail bounds. For $|t|\le d/2$, Taylor expansion plus $\epsilon\le d^{-k}$ moment closeness gives $O(d/(k2^{2k}))$. For $|t|\ge d/h$, convolving both densities with a band-limited mollifier (e.g. $\mathrm{sinc}^4$) kills the Fourier tail at TV cost $h\|f'\|_{L^1}$ — hence the need for the derivative bound. The intermediate band is handled by mapping the half-plane $\mathrm{Re}\,s\ge -s_1 d$ to the unit disk and applying the three-circle theorem, yielding $\le de^{-\Omega(kh^2)}/h$. Optimizing $h\sim\sqrt{\log k/k}$ gives the result.

**Why it matters** This puts the standard Porter–Thomas assumption underlying LXEB, certified randomness protocols, and quantum-advantage claims on rigorous footing, and gives transferable tools (three-circle interpolation plus mollification) for converting design properties into distributional closeness.

**Caveats** The depth is far above the experimentally relevant log/linear regime — nontrivial TV requires superlinear depth, and the conjecture is that the bounds are loose. The result concerns the distribution over circuits for one fixed bitstring, not the empirical bitstring distribution of a single circuit. Noise is not treated (left as an explicit open question), the analysis is brickwork-specific, and the mollification theorem assumes a monotone decreasing, absolutely continuous density. Inverse-exponential TV bounds appear out of reach with these methods.

## 3. Sublinear-depth Quantum Simulation of Electrons with Atomic Orbitals

[arXiv:2610.00950](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00950) · [SciRate](https://scirate.com/arxiv/2610.00950)

*Jakob Günther, Aram W. Harrow*

**TL;DR** — The authors show that a Trotter step for a molecular electronic-structure Hamiltonian expressed in localized atomic orbitals can be executed in polylogarithmic depth with *zero* ancillas, and that the full time evolution costs $N^{(2d-1)/d}t\,(Nt/\epsilon)^{o(1)}$ gates in depth $N^{(d-1)/d}t\,(Nt/\epsilon)^{o(1)}$. In 3D this is $N^{5/3+o(1)}$ gates at $N^{2/3+o(1)}$ depth — matching the best known scaling in any basis (first-quantized real space) while using chemistry's native, compact basis, and improving on prior atomic-orbital results by several powers of $N$ (previous best upper bound $\tilde O(N^3)$).

**The big picture** — Quantum chemistry on quantum computers has faced an awkward split: the basis sets chemists actually use are compact and physically meaningful but produce Hamiltonians with a quartic number of unstructured terms, while plane-wave and grid bases give tidy, cheap Hamiltonians at the cost of needing far more basis functions. This work closes that gap by proving that chemists' orbitals, after a standard symmetric orthogonalization, really are exponentially localized in a way that survives the large-molecule limit, and by exploiting the same hierarchical far-field structure that makes classical fast multipole methods fast. The payoff is a simulation whose circuit depth grows only as the two-thirds power of system size with no ancilla qubits, so the algorithm is both cheap and massively parallel.

**Key contributions**
- A rigorous definition of an "atomic-orbital set" (exponential gradient decay, Gram matrix spectral gap $A>0$, minimum nuclear separation $\delta$) and the MESH Hamiltonian family.
- **Theorem:** Löwdin ($S^{-1/2}$) orthogonalization preserves exponential localization with decay constants independent of the number of nuclei — apparently the first such proof for molecular orbital localization, built on Jaffard/Benzi matrix-decay theory.
- A fast-multipole-*inspired* Hamiltonian decomposition (used only to choose terms, not executed quantumly), plus Vizing edge coloring into 1-sparse one-electron operators.
- A polylog-depth, dirty-ancilla circuit for rank-one $ZZ$ interactions (depth $O(\log\frac{Nt}{\epsilon}\log N)$), closing a gap where only gate counts were previously claimed.

**How it works** — Orbital truncation to radius $r_{\max}=O(\zeta^{-1}\log\frac{N}{\epsilon})$ makes the overlap graph degree $\Delta=O((\log(N/\epsilon)/\zeta\delta)^d)$. Long-range Coulomb is split over $J=\tilde O(\log\frac N\epsilon)$ octree levels; each box-pair block gets a multipole expansion truncated at order $\kappa=O(\log\frac N\epsilon)$, yielding sums of products of one-electron operators. Edge coloring plus Jordan–Wigner and ancilla-free fermionic permutations (depth $O(\log^2 N)$) map each product to a rank-one $ZZ$ term, implemented by Fourier-arithmetic phase multiplication into idle system qubits used as dirty ancillas. Trotter error uses the Childs et al. commutator bound, giving error $O(8^k (k+1)! t^{k+1} N^{(k+1)(d-1)/d+1})$; optimizing $k$ gives the $o(1)$ exponents.

**Why it matters** — It removes the long-standing argument that atomic orbitals are asymptotically uncompetitive, and the ancilla-free, sublinear-depth structure is attractive for early fault-tolerant parallel architectures — qubitization cannot go sublinear in depth because of its $\Omega(\lambda t)$ query depth.

**Caveats** — Purely asymptotic: constants, polylog factors, and the $(Nt/\epsilon)^{o(1)}$ term (which hides factors exponential in Trotter order $k$ and quadratic in $\kappa$ and $\Delta$) are unoptimized, and no crossover system size is estimated. Results apply only to the thermodynamic limit with constant orbitals per atom $\kappa$ — the continuum/basis-set limit is explicitly excluded since $\lambda_{\min}(S)\to0$. The explicit lower bound on $A$ requires on-site orthogonality and sufficiently large $\delta\zeta$, degrading rapidly otherwise. Only time evolution is costed, not state preparation or the full energy-estimation pipeline, and whether the $5/3$ exponent is optimal is left open.

## 4. Learning SYK Hamiltonians

[arXiv:2610.02178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02178) · [SciRate](https://scirate.com/arxiv/2610.02178)

*Anurag Anshu, Srinivasan Arunachalam, Sitan Chen, Yeongwoo Hwang*

**TL;DR** — The authors give the first Hamiltonian-learning guarantees for the dense SYK model from its Gibbs state, where every quartic term overlaps Θ(n³) others and standard locality/bounded-degree machinery fails. At any fixed constant β they prove poly(n, 1/ε) sample complexity (n^{O(1+β²)}ε^{-2}log(n/ζ)) for recovering all couplings to ℓ₂ error ε, w.h.p. over disorder; and for sufficiently small constant β they give a quasipolynomial-time algorithm (n^{O(log n)} preprocessing + Õ(n¹⁴) runtime, n¹³log(n/ζ) samples) achieving normalized per-coupling error n^{-c}.

**The big picture** — Reconstructing the interaction parameters of a quantum many-body system from thermal samples is the quantum analogue of learning a graphical model, and every prior efficient algorithm has leaned on some form of locality or bounded interaction degree. The canonical model of quantum chaos — a dense, random, all-to-all fermionic model — violates both assumptions by a wide margin, and had been out of reach. This work shows that randomness and mean-field structure can substitute for locality: the disorder average itself provides the control that geometric locality normally supplies. That is both a conceptual data point about whether strongly chaotic systems hide their parameters, and a template for learning other disordered quantum models.

**Key contributions**
- First constant-temperature learning guarantee for a dense, random, non-commuting ensemble; sample complexity polynomial despite polynomial interaction degree. Extends to general even q with explicit exponents.
- A simplification of the Anshu–Arunachalam–Kuai–Yao maximum-entropy argument: exact coefficient extraction via a Majorana twirl, with no degree loss, plus a direct BKM-to-ordinary-variance comparison.
- A disorder-averaged bound on the local-quench Petz Rényi power via iterated commutator moments M₂ₖ ≤ [8k(1+β)]^{2k}, replacing a Kotecký–Preiss cluster expansion and extending the result to all β>0.
- A symmetry-based quasipolynomial algorithm that calibrates by inverting a 2×2 matrix, plus a strengthened weighted estimate ‖Δ_β‖₂,ₛ ≤ Cₛ/n on partition-function fluctuations.

**How it works** — Sample efficiency reduces to strong convexity of log Z at the true couplings: the Hessian equals the BKM variance, lower-bounded by the ordinary variance up to 1+βΔ with spectral diameter Δ = O(n). The Majorana twirl P_S extracts u_S exactly, reducing the variance bound to comparing ρ_β with its conjugate by Γ_A; the ratio of annealed partition functions is shown ≤ 8/3, then transferred to quenched via concentration and Markov, yielding λₙ ≥ n^{-O_β(1)}. Hessian Lipschitzness extends convexity to a ball around the target. For time efficiency, rescaled thermal expectations equal g plus an O(n^{-1/2}) quadratic bias; sign-flip and permutation covariance force all degree-≤2 corrections into a 2-dimensional span, so cancelling the bias needs only two calibration constants. Degree-O(log n) Taylor truncation plus L⁴ control of the proxy and n^{-(d-1)/2} decay of higher Hermite degrees close the argument.

**Why it matters** — Relevant to Hamiltonian learning theory, quantum simulator calibration, and the growing rigorous-algorithms literature on SYK; it parallels recent classical results on learning Sherrington–Kirkpatrick couplings.

**Caveats** — The sample-efficient result is information-theoretic: the max-entropy program is not known to be solvable efficiently, and strong convexity is only established at the (random) true point, not uniformly, blocking gradient descent. The time-efficient algorithm requires β below an explicit small constant, runs in quasipolynomial (not polynomial) time due to preprocessing, and achieves only normalized error n^{-c}, not inverse-polynomial ℓ₂ accuracy. β must be known, guarantees hold only with probability 1−O(1/n) over disorder, and the quasipolynomial result depends on inherited zero-freeness/thermal estimates.

## 5. Exponential quantum advantages for decoded quantum interferometry in the streaming setting

[arXiv:2610.01902](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01902) · [SciRate](https://scirate.com/arxiv/2610.01902)

*Kewen Wu, Guangxu Yang*

**TL;DR** The paper shows that decoded quantum interferometry (DQI), adapted to the one-pass streaming model, gives *unconditional* quantum memory advantages for a natural polynomial-fitting optimization problem. For a Hermite/multiplicity-code generalization of optimal polynomial intersection (OPI), a one-pass quantum streamer with polylog space and polylog per-entry time outputs a polynomial satisfying ~93.3% of the optimum, while any classical algorithm achieving even 75.001% needs polynomial space even with polynomially many passes and unlimited time. A matching classical upper bound completes the full approximation–space tradeoff curve.

**The big picture** Quantum algorithms for optimization usually claim speedups that are only conjectural, because nobody can rule out a clever classical algorithm. Here the authors switch the resource of interest from time to memory and passes over the data, where unconditional lower bounds are provable. They show that a recently proposed quantum optimization algorithm, when run on data arriving as a stream, finds good solutions using exponentially less memory than any classical method, no matter how much computation time it is given or how many times it rereads the input. Since memory is the scarcest resource in fault-tolerant machines, this is a concrete, verifiable form of quantum advantage for a natural optimization problem.

**Key contributions**
- Hermite OPI (HOPI): generalizes OPI from Reed–Solomon to univariate multiplicity codes, prescribing length-ℓ jets (Hasse derivatives) rather than single values.
- One-pass quantum streaming DQI: O(qℓ log q) space, poly(q,ℓ) time, achieving any α < α⋆ = (2+√3)/4 ≈ 0.93301.
- A near-optimal variant reaching any α < 1 with the same space and poly update time, but exponential postprocessing.
- Classical lower bound: any P-pass algorithm with α > 3/4 needs space ≳ q^{1+ℓ·κ(α)}/P, κ(α) = (4α−3)/(4α−2) — and a nearly matching one-pass classical upper bound, plus an O(qℓ log q)-space classical algorithm for α ≤ 3/4.
- A new list-recovery bound for multiplicity codes via hidden-derivative interpolation plus Kopparty's differential-root bound.

**How it works** The quantum side prepares, in one pass, the product state ⊗ᵢ(√(1−λ)|0⟩+√λ|ĥᵢ⟩), where phases encode membership bits; entries (i,z,𝟙[z∈Sᵢ]) only flip phases, so no input storage is needed. Projecting onto errors of weight ≤ d ≈ n/4, computing the syndrome, uncomputing the error via efficient unique decoding, and inverse Fourier transforming yields amplitudes ∝ P(score(u)), so measurement samples a polynomial with score concentrated at ½+√(λ(1−λ)); λ→¼ gives α⋆. For α→1 (λ→½) unique decoding fails, so the syndrome is augmented with the error on a random r = o(n) subset; a 2^{o(n)} list bound makes the augmented syndrome uniquely identifying, with exhaustive (space-cheap) decoding and a loss of ≤ r constraints. The classical bound goes through k = Θ(log q)-party communication with gadgetless lifting / structure-vs-randomness: list recovery caps the number of "dangerous" codewords exposed within a codimension budget T, while each newly dangerous codeword still has ~(1−1/k−ζ)n unexposed, pseudorandom queried bits, making it a solution with probability 2^{−Ω(n/log²n)}.

**Why it matters** This is a rare unconditional separation for a natural optimization problem, and the first where a *one-pass* quantum algorithm beats *many-pass* classical ones — unlike prior Max-DiCut/Max-2SAT streaming advantages, which constant-pass classical algorithms matched. The table of code families (Reed–Solomon, multiplicity, folded RS, LDPC, …) suggests a coding-theoretic taxonomy of Regev-reduction advantages.

**Caveats** The separation is for streaming space/passes, not time: the original OPI time advantage remains heuristic. Exponential space advantage requires ℓ growing polynomially in q, where the time advantage degrades to quasipolynomial. Lower bounds hold for random instances in a fixed grouped stream order. The near-optimal regime needs exponential postprocessing; whether polynomial-time postprocessing suffices is open, as is matching the 1/P pass dependence classically.
