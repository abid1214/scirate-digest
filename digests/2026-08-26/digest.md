## 1. Optimal Lower Bound for Ground-State Energy Estimation with a Guiding State

[arXiv:2608.24493](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24493) · [SciRate](https://scirate.com/arxiv/2608.24493)

*Rolando D. Somma, Ronald de Wolf*

**TL;DR** The paper proves an Ω(log(1/ε)/(γδ)) lower bound on the number of applications of U=e^{iH} (and U⁻¹) needed to estimate a ground-state energy to ±δ with failure probability ε, given a guiding state of overlap ≥γ — tight in all three parameters simultaneously, matching the concurrent Jeffery–Witteveen upper bound. The bound is proved via a fractional-query version of the polynomial method, and extends to instances with a unique gapped ground state, to ground-state *preparation*, to block-encoding access, and (as Ω(log(1/ε)/(γ√δ))) to sum-of-squares spectral amplification.

**The big picture** Estimating the lowest energy of a quantum system, starting from a rough guess state that overlaps the true ground state, is the workhorse subroutine of quantum chemistry algorithms, and the dominant cost is time evolution. Previous work pinned down the optimal cost only when one of the three quality parameters — precision, overlap, failure probability — was held fixed; in particular, no one knew whether the cost of driving the failure probability down could be cheapened, as it can for quantum search. This paper shows it cannot: the naive strategy of repeating and taking a median is already optimal, and the same is true for actually preparing the ground state rather than just its energy. Combined with a concurrent matching algorithm, the complexity of the guided Hamiltonian problem is now completely settled in the black-box time-evolution model.

**Key contributions**
- Joint lower bound Ω(log(1/ε)/(γδ)), for dimension ≥ log(1/ε)/γ².
- Same bound for a hard family with a *unique* ground state separated by gap 3δ (dimension log²(1/ε)/γ²), answering the more physically relevant version.
- Transfer to ground-state preparation (ε = trace distance), block-encoding access, and fractional/continuous-time queries.
- Ω(log(1/ε)/(γ√δ)) for SOSSA-style access to G with H=G†G, showing that spectral amplification's quadratic speedup in δ is essentially optimal.

**How it works** A fractional query e^{i3δx_j} is expanded as a combination of identity and an ordinary phase oracle; truncating the 2^T-term expansion after K = O(Tδ + log(1/ε)) oracle uses stays within ε in operator norm, so acceptance probability becomes a degree-O(Tδ+log(1/ε)) polynomial. Symmetrization plus Coppersmith–Rivlin (integer-grid to interval bound) and Chebyshev extremality give ε_D ≥ 2^{-O(D²/N + Dγ)}; setting N = log(1/ε)/γ² yields the bound. Conceptually: a T-query δ-precision algorithm yields a Tδ-query constant-precision algorithm, importing the known OR bound of Buhrman et al. For the unique-ground-state family, U reflects (up to phase θ) about √a|0⟩ + ω√(1−a)|j⟩ with useless guiding state |0⟩; averaging over ω kills odd cross terms, giving a univariate polynomial in a, and a case split trades the Chebyshev bound against an Ω(√N/δ) search bound at N = log²(1/ε)/γ².

**Why it matters** Settles an open question from Mande–de Wolf and calibrates how much error reduction genuinely costs for multi-output subroutines, where transducer tricks don't apply. Relevant to anyone designing or costing quantum chemistry pipelines.

**Caveats** Lower bounds require large dimension (up to log²(1/ε)/γ²) and don't cover the sparse-access model, where d-dependence remains open. The hard instances' guiding states carry no real information about the ground state beyond formal overlap — the authors themselves flag this as evidence that overlap is the wrong problem formulation. The hard families and initial proofs were generated by ChatGPT 5.6 Pro and subsequently simplified by the authors.

## 2. Improved Quantum Codes with Transversal T Gates

[arXiv:2608.24000](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24000) · [SciRate](https://scirate.com/arxiv/2608.24000)

*Adam Wills*

**TL;DR** This paper enlarges the known parameter frontier for qubit CSS codes admitting a strictly transversal $T$ gate (physical $T^{\otimes n}$ → logical $T^{\otimes k}$, no Clifford correction) by replacing Reed–Muller codes with general 8-divisible *decreasing monomial* codes punctured at a *downward-closed* set of hypercube points. The key technical tool is a closed-form distance formula for such punctures, $d(\mathrm{punc}_\Gamma(C(\Delta))) = \min_{A\in\Delta}|\{V\subseteq \bar A: V\notin\Gamma\}|$. This yields the first constant-rate, growing-distance codes with transversal $T$ — parameters $[[n,(R+o(1))n,\Omega(2^{(c_R+o(1))\sqrt{\log_2 n}})]]$ for any $R\in(0,1/3)$ with $c_R=-\Phi^{-1}(2R/(1+R))$ — hence the first magic-state-distillation exponent $\gamma\to0$ for a transversal-$T$ code.

**The big picture** Fault-tolerant quantum computers need a non-Clifford gate, and the cheapest and most hardware-friendly candidate is the single-qubit T gate; codes that implement it by simply applying the gate to every physical qubit are the most economical way to get one. Progress on such codes had been frozen since 2017–2018, with the best families paying a rate penalty that made distillation overhead scale polynomially. By generalising the underlying classical algebraic codes and, crucially, choosing which coordinates to puncture in a structurally compatible way, the author obtains code families whose logical-qubit count grows in proportion to the block size while distance still grows — meaning the asymptotic overhead of magic state distillation can be driven toward zero using only single-qubit transversal gates.

**Key contributions**
- New closed-form minimum distance for decreasing monomial (polar-type) codes punctured at a downset, plus a matching "non-annihilation" condition for injectivity; independent classical-coding interest.
- Explicit family: weighted Reed–Muller downsets with weight vector $(1,\dots,1,h)$, punctured at low-Hamming-weight points of $[m-1]$, giving 8-divisible codes of much higher rate than $\mathrm{RM}(m,r)$ with $3r<m$.
- Improved polynomial-rate/distance frontier $\beta_{\text{explicit}}(\alpha)$ (vs. Hastings–Haah replication), and constant-rate regime for the first time.
- Randomised refinement: a random $t$-uniform hypergraph "protects" points from the puncture set (still a downset), improving $\beta_{\text{exist}}(\alpha)$ — e.g. $1/2$ for all $\alpha\le1/2$, and $1-2H_2^{-1}(\alpha)$ for $\alpha\ge H_2(1/3)$.
- Extension to $R_\ell=\mathrm{diag}(1,e^{2\pi i/2^\ell})$ and hence transversally *addressable* lower-hierarchy gates, which multi-qubit transversal constructions do not give for free.

**How it works** Standard $X$-generator-matrix formalism: an 8-divisible classical code $C$ satisfies the Ward-identity consequences that make the triple/pair/single-row weight congruences hold, so $\mathcal{Q}[G]$ has transversal $T$; puncturing $k$ coordinates $\Gamma$ (with $C^\perp$ having no codeword inside $\Gamma$) creates $k$ logical qubits, and $d = d(\mathrm{punc}_\Gamma(C^\perp))$. Choosing both $\Delta$ (which fixes 8-divisibility) and $\Gamma$ as downsets makes this distance exactly computable via $\min_{A\notin\Delta}|2^A\setminus\Gamma|$; protecting sets containing a hyperedge guarantees $|2^A\setminus\Gamma|\ge 2^{|A|-t}$ for large $A$.

**Why it matters** Relevant to distillation-scheme designers and to the active program on non-Clifford transversality in qLDPC codes, where $CCZ$ has dominated because $T$-gate theory was less developed; these constructions supply concrete high-rate divisible-code targets and give particularly fast T-to-T distillation (no Clifford correction).

**Caveats** Codes are not LDPC — intended for logical-level/concatenated use. In the constant-rate regime distance grows only as $2^{\Theta(\sqrt{\log n})}$ (sub-polynomial), so $\gamma\to0$ but not $\gamma=0$; the rate is capped at $1/3$. Larger $h$ helps only at high rate, so low-rate regimes do not improve on Hastings–Haah/Haah. Randomisation gives no gain at constant rate. Restricted to stabiliser CSS codes; the author speculates non-stabiliser codes could do better. Source truncated, so proofs of the main theorems were not fully verifiable here.

## 3. Optimal Quantum Algorithm for Ground-State Energy Estimation with a Guiding State

[arXiv:2608.24494](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24494) · [SciRate](https://scirate.com/arxiv/2608.24494)

*Stacey Jeffery, Freek Witteveen*

**TL;DR** — This paper closes the long-standing logarithmic gap in guided ground-state energy estimation: it gives an algorithm that estimates the maximum eigenphase of a unitary $U$ to precision $\delta$, given a guiding state with ground-space overlap $\gamma$, using $O(1/(\gamma\delta))$ (controlled) queries to $U$ and $U^\dagger$ — matching the known $\Omega(1/(\gamma\delta))$ lower bound — plus only $O(\frac{1}{\gamma}\log\frac{1}{\delta}\log\log\frac{1}{\delta})$ calls to the state-preparation unitary $A$. The mechanism is a purpose-built *transducer* (Belovs' Las Vegas / catalytic model of quantum computation) for a decision version of phase estimation composed with one for amplitude amplification, which avoids the error-reduction overhead that forced $\log\frac{1}{\gamma}$ in both QPE- and QSVT-based approaches.

**The big picture** — Estimating the lowest energy of a quantum system, starting from a trial state that has some guaranteed overlap with the true ground state, is the flagship fault-tolerant quantum application in chemistry and materials science. Every prior method paid a logarithmic penalty in the inverse overlap, because subroutine errors had to be suppressed below the tiny signal coming from the guiding state, and it was unclear whether this penalty was fundamental. Here the authors show it is not: by designing the algorithm in a compositional framework where subroutines can be combined exactly rather than approximately, they reach the known optimal cost. Since phase estimation is ubiquitous, the same trick plausibly removes similar overheads elsewhere.

**Key contributions**
- Optimal query complexity for guided max-eigenphase / ground-state energy estimation; combined with a concurrent lower bound (Somma et al.) this pins the complexity at $\Theta(\frac{1}{\gamma\delta}\log\frac1\epsilon)$ for large enough dimension.
- A simple exact transducer for *decision amplitude amplification* (walk on a weighted star graph): transduction complexity $w/\epsilon$ if $\epsilon>0$, $1/w$ if $\epsilon=0$; setting $w=\gamma$ gives $W\le 1/\gamma$.
- An infinite-dimensional transducer for *decision phase estimation*: a quantum walk on weighted half-lines with ratio $\gamma_k=\tan(\theta_k/2)$, implemented via reflections built from $R(U)=\ket{-}\bra{+}\otimes e^{i\sigma}U+\text{h.c.}$
- A general truncation lemma (exact $(D,m)$-truncations) letting one replace the infinite counter register by a $O(\log D)$-qubit modular counter with *zero* error.
- Explicit use of a canonical-form oracle $O_\gamma(A)$ so that Las Vegas query complexity $L\le 1+1/\gamma$ decouples $A$-calls from $U$-calls.

**How it works** — The composed transducer $S=S^\circ O_\gamma(A)$ acts on public space $\mathrm{span}\{\ket{\bar 1,0}\}$ and maps it to $+\ket{\bar1,0}$ or $-\ket{\bar1,0}$ depending on whether $\|\Pi_{>s}\ket\psi\|\ge\gamma$ or $\Pi_{>s-\delta'}\ket\psi=0$, with $W=O(1/(\gamma\delta))$, $L\le 1+1/\gamma$. Single-bit phase estimation reads out the sign; binary search over $s$ gives the estimate.

**Why it matters** — Removes a genuine constant-in-practice overhead (e.g. $\log\frac1\gamma$ can be 10–20 for realistic chemistry instances) from the dominant cost, time evolution for total time $O(\lambda/(\gamma\delta))$. It is also the clearest demonstration yet that transducers yield concrete asymptotic wins in a mainstream algorithmic task.

**Caveats** — Extra space overhead $O(\log\frac{1}{\gamma\delta})$ vs. $O(\log\frac1\delta)$ standard; $A$-call count still carries $\log\frac1\delta\log\log\frac1\delta$; constants unoptimized and no resource estimate given; requires the window promise (eigenphases in $[0,\pi/2]$); the outer binary search means the routine is not obviously fully coherent, which the authors flag as open.

## 4. Nearly Optimal Amplitude Estimation at any Depth

[arXiv:2608.24434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24434) · [SciRate](https://scirate.com/arxiv/2608.24434)

*Jona Erle, Bálint Koczor*

**TL;DR** The paper introduces Windowed Least-Squares Amplitude Estimation (WLSAE): sample Grover-power depths $m$ from an arbitrary "admissible" window distribution on $\{-M,\dots,M\}$, collect $\pm1$ outcomes with $\mathbb{E}[Z_m]=\cos(2m\lambda)$, and fit $\lambda$ by minimizing a one-dimensional least-squares loss over a grid. Any admissible window provably achieves $M^2N\in\tilde{\mathcal O}(\epsilon^{-2})$ additive error in the *Grover angle* uniformly over $\lambda\in[0,\pi/2]$ — including the boundaries $\lambda\to0,\pi/2$, where all prior depth-tunable schemes degrade to classical sampling — with no ancillas and no controlled Grover operators.

**The big picture** Amplitude estimation is the workhorse behind quadratic speedups in quantum Monte Carlo, finance and chemistry, but the textbook version needs deep coherent circuits, an ancilla register and a Fourier transform. A line of recent work replaces these with shallow randomized circuits plus classical fitting, letting you dial circuit depth against repetition count; the catch is that existing guarantees quietly break down when the amplitude being estimated is very close to zero or one — precisely the regime of overlap certification and trial-state verification. This work shows that the breakdown was an artifact of the analysis, not the algorithms: a broad, easily-satisfied family of depth-sampling distributions retains the full quantum advantage everywhere, and simpler distributions than the previously proposed Gaussian actually perform better.

**Key contributions**
- A general class of *admissible windows*: mass condition (W1) $p(m)+p(-m)\ge b/T$ for $1\le m\le T$ with $T=\max(1,M/\sigma)$, plus moment bounds (W2); Gaussian GLSAE is one member.
- A uniform guarantee for all $\lambda\in[0,\pi/2]$, matching the Zalka–Burchard bound $M^2N\in\Omega(\epsilon^{-2})$ up to $\log(\epsilon^{-1}\delta^{-1})$; hence the boundary "fallback to classical sampling" patch of GLSAE is unnecessary.
- Interpolation corollary: $M=\lfloor\epsilon^{-1+\beta}\rfloor$ gives $N\in\mathcal O(\epsilon^{-2\beta}\log)$ and $MN\in\mathcal O(\epsilon^{-1-\beta}\log)$ queries, continuously spanning $\beta\in[0,1]$.
- Numerics comparing Gaussian, Kaiser, uniform, linear, cubic windows at $Q=10^6$: the *uniform* window beats the Gaussian, with $\epsilon_{95}$ close to the Cramér–Rao bound.

**How it works** The proof sandwiches the population loss between $C_2\Psi^2$ and $C_1\Psi^2$ with envelope $\Psi(\Delta)=\min(1,T\Delta,T^2\Delta\max(\bar\lambda,\Delta))$, $\bar\lambda=\min(\lambda,\pi/2-\lambda)$ — the boundary-aware factor absent from earlier analyses. Lower bounds use Dirichlet-kernel estimates for $|\theta-\lambda|\ge\pi/2T$ and explicit sums of $m^2\sin^2(ms)$ near the minimum. A Bernstein bound plus union over $K$ grid points controls the empirical fluctuation by $\max(r\Psi,\sqrt r\,\Omega)$; crucially the shot-noise variance $\sin^2(2m\lambda)$ vanishes at the boundary at the same rate as the loss curvature, so the signal-to-noise ratio is preserved. Localization then follows from the minimizer argument.

**Why it matters** Gives early-fault-tolerant practitioners an ancilla-free, control-free, depth-tunable AE primitive with a clean uniform guarantee and design freedom over the window — relevant for overlap/fidelity certification where amplitudes are near 1.

**Caveats** The explicit constants are enormous ($A>3\times10^6$, so the grid $K=\lceil A\pi/2\epsilon\rceil$ and the sample bound $\propto A^2C_9^2$ are wildly loose); practical performance rests on the numerics, not the bound. Guarantees are for the grid minimizer (cost $O(NK)$ naively), assume noiseless circuits, and the experiments shown are at a single $\lambda=0.5$ and one query budget.

## 5. Essentially optimal gate teleportation

[arXiv:2608.24345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24345) · [SciRate](https://scirate.com/arxiv/2608.24345)

*Lukas Schmitt, David Sutter*

**TL;DR** The authors give the first deterministic LOCC protocol that teleports a nonlocal two-qubit gate using strictly less than one ebit: for the controlled-phase gate with angle φ, a Schmidt-rank-3 resource state with entanglement entropy h(q) + (1−q), where q = 1/(1+sin(φ/2)), suffices, and no protocol can do better than h(q). Since the additive gap (1−q) vanishes with φ, the cost is essentially optimal for small angles — small rotations need only a little entanglement — and this turns a distributed QFT on 2n qubits from n² ebits into a constant ≈12.19 ebits.

**The big picture** Two distant parties can perform a joint quantum gate without ever interacting directly, by consuming pre-shared entanglement plus classical messages. Until now every known exact, deterministic protocol burned at least one full unit of entanglement, even for gates that barely entangle anything — an obvious waste that had been flagged as an open problem for two decades. This work exhibits an explicit protocol whose entanglement consumption scales down smoothly with how weakly entangling the gate is, together with a nearly matching impossibility bound, and shows the payoff is dramatic for distributed algorithms built from many tiny rotations.

**Key contributions**
- Explicit four-round LOCC protocol (4 classical bits) using a qutrit-level resource state, breaking the one-ebit barrier for the first time.
- Matching-to-leading-order converse via Nielsen majorization; a strictly stronger converse for resource states of fixed Schmidt rank D.
- Extensions to arbitrary controlled two-qubit gates and, via KAK, to all two-qubit unitaries; constant-cost distributed QFT.

**How it works** *Achievability:* the resource state √q|00⟩ + √((1−q)/2)(|11⟩+|22⟩) is used in a chain of controlled operations. Alice's first controlled unitary plus a measurement collapses A′ to a two-dimensional subspace with outcome probabilities exactly 1/2 (so no information leaks about her input). Bob then applies a permutation, a diagonal phase D_x, and a qutrit Fourier transform before measuring — the Fourier basis prevents his outcome from revealing Alice's branch. The two phases d_i, d_j are chosen as intersection points of two circles in the complex plane, precisely the condition making Alice's round-3 correction unitary; this yields the feasibility constraint sin²(φ/2)(1−2p₁)(1−2p₂) ≤ 4p₁p₂, and an entropy argument shows the symmetric p₁=p₂ point is the optimum. *Converse:* running the protocol on the witness input (|00⟩+|01⟩−e^{iφ}|10⟩+|11⟩)/2, whose Schmidt spectrum ((1±sin(φ/2))/2) is mapped to a maximally entangled output, plus Nielsen majorization forces p₀ ≤ q, hence H ≥ h(q). The tighter converse uses SEP product Kraus operators, Binet–Cauchy determinant expansion of Ê Ψ F̂ᵀ = α Û, and Cauchy–Schwarz to bound the tail mass.

**Why it matters** Directly relevant to distributed/modular quantum computing and circuit knitting, where entanglement distribution is the bottleneck: the QFT example converts a quadratic entanglement budget into an n-independent constant.

**Caveats** Achievability and converse do not coincide except asymptotically as φ→0; for larger φ one falls back on the known one-ebit protocol. General two-qubit unitaries are handled by teleporting the three KAK factors separately, which the authors admit is likely suboptimal (and can exceed two ebits). Entanglement entropy of a single resource state is the cost metric, justified by asymptotic LOCC interconvertibility rather than exact single-shot resource accounting. The paper acknowledges LLM assistance in deriving the protocol and the strengthened converse.
